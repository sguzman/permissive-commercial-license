## Executive conclusion

The narrow policy can be expressed as a copyright permission with a defined commercial-distribution exception. Draft 1 is directionally consistent with that policy. It should not be finalized until high-priority issues concerning the scope of copyright, Mexican statutory rights and written licensing formalities, and mandatory consumer liability have been addressed. Its exact commercial-substitution test has no established judicial interpretation identified in this review.

## Legal analysis

### Mexico: copyright and electronic formalities

The Federal Copyright Law (LFDA), articles 19 and 24, preserves moral rights separately from economic exploitation. Article 26 bis provides a nonwaivable author right to remuneration for public communication or transmission. Article 30 distinguishes transfers from exclusive and nonexclusive use licenses and requires written form for use licenses. Articles 31–33 contain rules on transfers, including remuneration and duration; those transfer rules should not automatically be treated as applying identically to nonexclusive licenses. PCL grants a nonexclusive permission, not a transfer of ownership.

The Federal Civil Code (CCF), articles 1803 and 1834 bis, recognizes electronic consent and electronic satisfaction of certain writing requirements, subject to conditions such as attribution and later accessibility. This does not establish that a public repository alone satisfies all LFDA formalities. Counsel should assess the precise requirements for the public grant, downstream recipients and whether any registration/formalities are relevant. Keep dated releases, exact license text, ownership evidence and attributable notices. Do not require recipients to sign a separate contract unless counsel identifies a need; that would change the frictionless policy.

Proposed wording for consideration (not incorporated): "No royalty is payable under this License for the licensed rights, except to the extent that applicable law confers a right to remuneration that cannot lawfully be waived. This License does not transfer ownership or waive nonwaivable moral rights."

Sources: [LFDA via WIPO Lex, consolidated July 2020, arts. 19, 24, 26 bis, 30–33](https://cdn.nestjs.wipolex.wji.prd.web1.wipo.int/wipolex/es/legislation/details/20225); [2026 consolidation listing via Justia](https://mexico.justia.com/federales/leyes/ley-federal-del-derecho-de-autor/); [CCF electronic transaction reform, official DOF](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/2055516). The historical WIPO text is not a complete verification of all subsequent amendments; counsel must confirm current provisions and their interpretation before finalization.

### US: license conditions and copyright scope

Jacobsen v. Katzer, 535 F.3d 1373 (Fed. Cir. 2008), recognized certain public-license conditions as limitations on the copyright grant. MDY Industries v. Blizzard, 629 F.3d 928 (9th Cir. 2010, amended 2011), distinguishes infringement from contract claims and requires a connection between a condition and exclusive copyright rights for that circuit's infringement analysis. These decisions are illustrative, not rulings on PCL.

Section 3 should regulate licensed acts such as reproduction, distribution and public communication of copied protected material, not independent competing functionality or lawful resale of a particular copy under applicable exhaustion rules. The grant should clearly state its limitations; no legal conclusion about a specific clone is possible without facts. Distinguish the relevant covered work (engine versus game, clip versus whole episode) and assess the overall offering rather than effort or arbitrary changes. A competitor making a materially distinct product should remain permitted.

Sources: [Jacobsen, full opinion](https://openjurist.org/535/f3d/1373/jacobsen-v-katzer); [MDY, full amended opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/09-15932/09-15932-2011-02-25.html).

### Mexico and EU: consumer protections and liability

CCF article 2106 prohibits waiver of liability arising from intentional wrongdoing; article 2117 permits contractual regulation of liability subject to statutory limits. Mexico's Federal Consumer Protection Law (LFPC), article 90, invalidates certain clauses of adhesion contracts that improperly release suppliers from or shift civil liability. A public copyright grant is not a universal replacement for a paid product's consumer terms.

EU Directive 2019/770, article 22, protects mandatory consumer rights for covered contracts for digital content or services. Article 3(5)(f) has a specific exception for certain free/open-source software offerings; PCL should not assume that it qualifies. Applicability depends on transaction, jurisdiction, national transposition, price and data processing, among other facts. A separate sale or service agreement and product-specific compliance may be needed. No license can prevent filing a lawsuit.

Sources: [CCF arts. 2106, 2117](https://mexico.justia.com/federales/codigos/codigo-civil-federal/libro-cuarto/primera-parte/titulo-cuarto/i/incumplimiento-de-las-obligaciones/capitulo-i/); [LFPC official HTML, art. 90 (historical consolidation)](https://www.ordenjuridico.gob.mx/Documentos/Federal/html/wo12974.html); [EU Directive 2019/770, arts. 3 and 22](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/770/oj/eng).

### Comparison with established license drafting

Apache 2.0 uses a defined patent grant, warranty disclaimer, limitation of liability, and separate obligations when a redistributor offers warranties on its own behalf. CC BY 4.0 expressly addresses moral rights, statutory exceptions, downstream terms, royalties, disclaimer and termination/reinstatement. These provide drafting reference points, not compatibility or enforceability assurances. PCL's commercial-substitution restriction is not authorized by MIT or CC BY, and fails the unrestricted redistribution criterion of the Open Source Definition. Do not call it OSI-approved.

Sources: [Apache 2.0](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0.html); [CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en); [Open Source Definition](https://opensource.org/osd).

## Policy regression cases for a later draft

These are intended outcomes, not court predictions. All applicable attribution requirements and third-party rights remain relevant.

| Case | Intended result |
| --- | --- |
| Original game, paid third-party storefront, only logo swapped | Not authorized when direct substitute |
| Same source code, independent game with original assets and gameplay | Permitted |
| Same game plus substantial original levels and features | Permitted if independent value is material |
| Independently implemented algorithm with similar output | Outside PCL restriction |
| Free, unmonetized competing mirror | Permitted |
| Free download gated by paid subscription or mandatory donation | Assess as commercial distribution; prohibit if unchanged substitute |
| Original video uploaded unchanged for directly attributable ad revenue | Not authorized when direct substitute |
| Monetized critique incorporating excerpts and substantial commentary | Permitted subject to rights |
| Translation to reach a new language audience | Fact-specific; no categorical commercial prohibition |
| Genuine hosting of software with no prohibited distribution | Permitted |
| Sold support for unmodified software | Permitted, unless disguised substitute charge |
| Lawful resale of a particular copy under applicable exhaustion rules | Independent statutory rights preserved |
| Paid game causes data loss | Disclaimer and liability limits apply only as law allows; not automatic immunity |
