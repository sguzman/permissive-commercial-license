# Frequently asked questions and examples

This document explains intended operation of the [PCL 1.0 Draft 2](PCL-1.0-draft.2.txt). It does not add permissions or restrictions to the actual license text.

## Is this a noncommercial license?

No. Commercial internal use, ordinary commercial component reuse, meaningful commercial derivatives, and genuine paid services are permitted. Section 3 concerns commercial distribution of substantially unchanged direct substitutes only.

## Does it prevent competitors from using my code?

Not merely because they compete. Cloning the repository, compiling it and working on modifications are permitted, including for commercial development. A materially improved product may compete with the original. Independent implementation of an idea is not restricted by copyright permissions. A substantially unchanged commercially distributed substitute is not authorized.

## Can a third party sell my original work?

Sometimes. Selling a copy that is not a commercial substitute is permitted, as are reasonable actual distribution costs. Offering essentially the same game to the same audience as a competing paid product is not authorized. Rights such as exhaustion or first sale that apply independently under law are unaffected.

## Can someone distribute the original free of charge?

Yes, subject to notices and other conditions, if distribution is not commercially monetized. This can include a free mirror that competes for attention or downloads. PCL deliberately does not prevent every possible loss of revenue. A monetized mirror requires analysis of the three-part test.

## What if a website has ads or donations?

Revenue directly attributable to the publication may make it commercial distribution. Ordinary unrelated ads or voluntary donations alone do not necessarily do so. A paid bundle giving access to a copy, a mandatory donation gate, a paywalled mirror or a directly ad-monetized reupload of the original work will normally satisfy the commercial element. The unchanged and substitution elements must also be met.

## What counts as meaningful modification?

Independent value for the recipient, not effort alone. Original levels, substantive features, new creative expression, materially valuable localization, and substantive accessibility improvements may qualify. Renaming, reformatting, changing metadata, or swapping a splash screen alone do not. There is no percentage safe harbor. Whether a particular adaptation qualifies depends on the facts.

## Is a translation allowed?

Translation is explicitly permitted. Noncommercial distribution is permitted subject to conditions. Commercial translation may be permitted if it provides material independent value or does not substitute for the original publication. A superficial automated translation is not automatically exempt from Section 3.

## Can someone create a closed-source fork?

Yes. There is no obligation to provide modified source. Covered portions retain their PCL permissions and notices; the author of new contributions may license those contributions separately. A proprietary license for new code does not remove PCL rights in covered material. The restriction on downstream terms does not require separate component delivery, new source disclosure, or removal of technical access controls.

## Can a hosting company charge for a service?

Yes for genuine hosting, integration, maintenance, or similar service. Ordinary hosted execution without distributing a prohibited commercial substitute is permitted. A paid download or monetized public delivery of the original protected content may qualify as commercial distribution. Distinguish payment for additional service from a disguised charge for a prohibited substitute.

## Can I use PCL for games and videos?

Yes, if you own or control the relevant rights. Identify exactly which code, assets, scripts, and finished works are covered. Separately licensed music, stock footage, dependencies, performer rights, platform terms, and trademarks may constrain what you can grant. PCL is a copyright license, not a replacement for a platform's terms.

## How should I license a YouTube video?

If you choose to grant PCL rights, use YouTube's standard license and include a clear additional license notice identifying the covered material and exact PCL version. Do not also select a broader Creative Commons grant unless that is intentional: recipients could rely on the broader grant. Platform terms still apply. Review the platform's current settings before publication.

## Can I reuse an MIT dependency in a PCL project?

You can generally incorporate MIT-licensed material while preserving its notices and original permissions. Do not claim that PCL revokes MIT rights in that dependency. Check all other licenses individually. GPL compatibility for a combined covered work is not established and is generally problematic because PCL adds a commercial redistribution restriction.

## Can I change licenses later?

You may choose a different license for future releases or offer alternative terms for material you own. Existing grants to compliant recipients remain in effect. You cannot withdraw previous MIT or PCL permissions merely by changing a repository's current license.

## Can a licensor still be sued?

Yes. The draft disclaims warranties and limits liability only to the maximum extent permitted by law. It does not eliminate mandatory consumer obligations, personal injury liability where nonwaivable, or separate contractual obligations. It has not been reviewed by counsel. Fraud and intentional misconduct liability are expressly preserved where applicable law forbids exclusion. A separate consumer sale or service agreement may be necessary.

## Is PCL open source?

No, in the conventional OSI sense. Source visibility and broad reuse are compatible with source-available licensing, but the commercial-substitute restriction does not meet the unrestricted redistribution requirement of the Open Source Definition. PCL is not OSI-approved.

## Where do I request additional permissions?

Contact the relevant copyright holder through their designated project channels. The maintainer of this specification cannot grant rights in other authors' works. Additional written permissions can override restrictions for material owned by the granting holder.
